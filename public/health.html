Health and safety notice

Last updated: 24 October 2026

Setpoint is not medical advice. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition and can’t replace a doctor, dietitian or qualified trainer.

Under 18? Please read this

Your plan is set to maintenance. If you are under 18, Setpoint doesn’t offer weight-loss targets or calorie deficits. Your daily calorie goal is an estimate of what your body needs to stay at the same weight while you keep growing. Young people need enough food for growth, bones, hormones and energy.

You can raise the recommended calories in Profile, then Nutrition. If you do, you do it at your own risk. Setpoint can’t see your health, growth, training load or medical history, so it can’t tell whether a higher target is right for you. Please talk to a parent or guardian, doctor or dietitian first.

You can’t set a target below the maintenance amount. BMI categories are not reliable for teenagers and Setpoint doesn’t use them to judge you.

For everyone

  • Check with a doctor before starting a new diet or exercise program, particularly if you have a medical condition, take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are recovering from injury.
  • If you have, or have had, an eating disorder, or find tracking calories makes you anxious or unwell, please stop and talk to a health professional. Tracking isn’t for everyone.
  • Numbers are estimates. Food databases, recipes, portions, photo recognition and calorie-burn formulas all have error.
  • Allergies: Setpoint’s filters match food names only. They can’t guarantee a recipe or ingredient is allergen-free. Always check packaging.
  • Exercise safely: warm up, use controlled technique, don’t train through pain, and use a spotter or safety bars for heavy lifts. Stop if you feel dizzy, faint, short of breath or have chest pain, and seek medical help if symptoms continue.
  • Very low calorie targets, rapid weight loss and extreme training are risky. Setpoint limits targets to a range, but that range isn’t a recommendation for you personally.

If you need support

If you are in crisis or worried about your safety, contact your local emergency number. For eating-disorder support in the US, the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline is available; in other countries search for your national eating-disorder or youth helpline. You can also speak to your GP.
